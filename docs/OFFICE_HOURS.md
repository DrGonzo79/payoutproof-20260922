# Office Hours: PayoutProof

Generated 2026-09-22 · Startup diagnostic · pre-product

## Specific customer

**Dana**, the owner of a 3–8-year-old mainstream SUV who has just received a total-loss offer and has 3–10 days to decide whether to challenge it. Dana is stressed, unfamiliar with valuation adjustments, and stands to lose thousands — but cannot justify a $500 appraisal before knowing a material gap exists.

## Demand reality

The source provides a large-claim-volume thesis and a favorable $99-to-thousands value ratio, but no proof of willingness to pay, retention, conversion, or recovered dollars. This is a **problem hypothesis**, not validated demand. The first evidence bar is 10 claimants sharing redacted valuation reports; the second is 5 paying for a manually assembled audit; the decisive signal is an adjuster revising an offer after receiving one.

## Painful status quo

Claimants currently accept the valuation, search broad book values that do not match insurer methodology, manually browse listings, argue by phone, or hire an appraiser. Manual browsing lacks normalized trim/mileage/year adjustments; professional review is slower and costly before the potential upside is known.

## Desperate use case

A recently totaled vehicle is the household’s primary transportation, the insurer has offered $25,400, comparable replacements appear closer to $29,000, and rental coverage is ending. The job is: **“Help me determine whether the valuation is materially wrong and give me credible questions to ask today.”**

## Narrowest paid wedge

A $29–$49 same-day “offer audit”: ingest or manually enter the vehicle and offer, normalize 3–6 transparent comparables, flag weak/mismatched evidence, and produce an editable clarification request. Do not promise a certified appraisal or payout increase. Test $99 only after real recovered-value evidence and professional review.

## Observation plan

Watch five claimants, without coaching, move from valuation PDF to their first insurer response. Record where they misread adjustments, which evidence they exclude, and whether they want a number or a defensible letter. The pivotal surprise to seek: whether users value comp discovery, error detection, or negotiation language most.

## Future-fit

As insurer valuation workflows become more automated, input quality and explainability become more important. PayoutProof becomes essential only if it owns a trustworthy normalization/audit trail; generic “AI says your car is worth X” becomes less credible and more commoditized.

## Premises

1. A rapid screening audit can create value before a licensed appraisal.
2. Transparent adjustment math builds more trust than an opaque predicted price.
3. Distribution at the post-accident moment matters more than broad consumer SEO.
4. Live listing rights and jurisdiction-specific claim rules are gating risks, not implementation details.

## Approaches considered

### A — Manual concierge audit (minimal viable)
- **Effort:** S · **Risk:** Low
- Analyst reviews a redacted valuation plus public comps and returns a structured memo.
- Fastest demand test and best source of workflow learning; does not scale and creates operational variance.

### B — Explainable self-serve audit (recommended prototype)
- **Effort:** M · **Risk:** Medium
- A claimant edits key inputs, includes/excludes comps, sees normalized values, and drafts a written request.
- Strong demo of the core trust loop; live data/licensing and document extraction remain deferred.

### C — Appraiser/attorney evidence platform (ideal architecture)
- **Effort:** XL · **Risk:** High
- Multi-jurisdiction rules, licensed reviewers, immutable evidence snapshots, outcomes tracking, and insurer-specific templates.
- Highest defensibility and revenue potential; premature without demand and partner validation.

## Recommendation and assignment

Build B as an interactive proof while selling A manually. **Assignment:** recruit five recent total-loss claimants and obtain consented, redacted valuation reports before adding PDF extraction or marketplace integrations.
